03 · Block 01 · Open

The Frame

Before we look at seven ways to think with AI, we need one formula, one model, and one lens. The first eight minutes set everything that follows.

The BI Formula

Business Impact = Data Context × AI Capability raised to the power of APT.
APT is the exponent. Not a multiplier. That matters.

BI = DX × (CAI)APT
When APT is weak, AI investment is mathematically neutralized
APT Value
Mathematical Effect
Business Reality
APT = 0
(CAI)0 = 1 → BI = DX
AI investment neutralized. Raw data, nothing else.
APT < 1
AI capability dampened
Worse than no AI. Output degrades below baseline.
APT = 1
BI = DX × CAI (linear)
Parity with competitors. No amplification.
APT > 1
Exponential amplification
Differentiated business impact — where the value is
Strategic Frame
You can buy the best AI and the richest data — but without investing in how your people think with AI, you get linear returns at best. APT is the cheapest lever with the highest exponential payoff.

The APT Model

Three dimensions. Multiplicative — not additive. Weakness in any one suppresses the whole.

APT = f(AI Skills × Domain Expertise × Thinking Framework)

AI Skills — Position Only

Three stacked layers. Today we mostly operate at the innermost — A.C.T.I.O.N.S. We touch CIS Prompt briefly. Agentic Prompting is out of scope.

The Lens — Dual Mode of Domain Expertise

Before any AI task, ask one question: Am I the Editor here, or the Student? Same person, same hour — different task means different mode.

P
Production Mode

The Editor

  • Domain Expertise is HIGH
  • You can verify the output
  • You can reject generic answers
  • You know what good looks like
  • Confident posture — full stack accessible
E
Exploration Mode

The Student

  • Domain Expertise is LOW
  • Cannot verify accuracy vs. plausibility
  • Learning, not deciding
  • Cautious posture — heavy Meta Prompting
  • Avoid agentic delegation
The Warning
Most AI failures by senior leaders happen when they think they are in Production Mode but are actually in Exploration Mode — and they don't notice.

Four Filters — In Production Mode

When you are the Editor, four filters catch four different kinds of failure. Three are partially compensable through prompt engineering. The fourth is not.

01
Relevance
Does the output reflect your org's actual context?
02
Excellence
Is this world-class, or just competent-sounding?
03
Reality
Is this operationally possible given constraints?
04
Trust
Where AI must stop and humans take over
★ Trust Filter — Non-Negotiable
The relational moment with a customer, the difficult conversation with a direct report, the trust-building meeting with a regulator — that is your job. AI prepares you for it. AI cannot do it for you.

01

Pre-Training · Search · Deep Research

Get briefed in minutes. Verify before you quote.
Exploration Mode Algorithmic Empathy + Dialectical Sparring

What it's for. Getting briefed on a domain before a meeting. Catching up on a market you haven't tracked in months. Building a fast baseline.

Why this is Exploration Mode. You don't know what you don't know. You cannot reliably separate accurate output from plausible-sounding output.

Sample Prompt I need a 5-minute executive briefing on the current state of Indonesia's EV battery supply chain — specifically the nickel-to- cathode integration plays. Who are the top three players, what are the key 2025-2026 milestones, and what are the biggest unresolved risks? Cite sources.
When NOT to use
When stakes are high and you cannot verify the output. Bring in a human domain expert instead.
02

Brainstorming

AI is creative. Your context is the unlock.
Either Mode Synthetic Sense-Making

What it's for. Generating ideas, options, frames. AI has inherent breadth — it has seen more brainstorming output than any human ever could.

The trap. The reason brainstorming with AI feels generic is almost always the same: your context payload is empty. Generic question → generic answer.

Bad — Empty Context
Give me ideas for our Q3 marketing campaign.
Good — Rich Context
We are a premium Indonesian halal F&B brand,
mid-market positioning, strongest in Greater
Jakarta and Bandung. Q3 2026 hits the
post-Lebaran lull. 2025 Q3 was 18% below H1
average. Want ideas that lean into household
routine moments without diluting premium
positioning. Ten distinct angles, then mark
the three you'd defend strongest and say why.
When NOT to use
When the brainstorm requires lived experience the AI doesn't have access to (team morale dynamics, specific stakeholder politics).
03

High-Quality Ideas — A.C.T.I.O.N.S. ★

Seven elements. One quality prompt. First time, not tenth time.
Production Mode Algorithmic Empathy

What it's for. When you need a quality output the first time, not after ten iterations.

A — Actor
Who should the AI act as? "A senior strategy consultant" not "a helpful assistant."
C — Context
What does the LLM not know on its own? Org, market, prior decisions, constraints.
T — Task
One clear verb. "Analyze." "Draft." "Compare." "Critique."
I — Intention
Why are you asking? What decision does this serve?
O — Output
Format, length, structure. Specific beats generic.
N — Negative
What should it NOT do? Most leaders skip this. Don't.
S — Sequences
If multi-part, what order? If single-shot, say so.
Landing Line
A.C.T.I.O.N.S. is not bureaucracy. It is the discipline of supplying the LLM what its character does not give you for free.
04

Sharpening — Iterate · COT · Discussion

First draft is the AI's average. Your job is to get it off its average.
Production Mode Dialectical Sparring

Three iteration moves — in sequence:

Move 01
Direct Iteration
"This is good but generic. Make it specific to manufacturing in West Java. Cut the corporate jargon."
Move 02
Chain-of-Thought
"Walk me through your reasoning step by step before giving the final answer."
Move 03
Forced Disagreement
"Now argue against your own recommendation. Three strongest counter-arguments."
When NOT to use
When you're stuck five iterations in. Step back — either your framing was wrong, or AI isn't suited for this task.
05

Decision Help — Full Stack

High stakes. Run the synthesis of everything before.
Production Mode All Four Frameworks

The five-step flow — for any decision that matters:

Step 01 · Synthetic Sense-Making
Build the Context Payload
Market data · Internal capability · Constraints · Strategic intent. Engineer the context as an artifact, not afterthought.
Step 02 · Algorithmic Empathy
Write the A.C.T.I.O.N.S. Prompt
Apply all seven elements. Especially Intention (why this decision) and Negative Constraints (what NOT to recommend).
Step 03 · Dialectical Sparring
Run the Iteration Loop
Steelman. Pre-mortem. Counter-argument. Forced specificity. Do not accept the first draft.
Step 04 · Domain Expertise
Apply the Four Filters
Relevance · Excellence · Reality · Trust. Each catches a different failure mode.
Step 05 · YOU
Decide — and Own It
AI does not make decisions. AI improves the quality of thinking that precedes a decision. The accountability is yours.
06

Thinking Based on Data — NotebookLM

Reasoning over your documents. Grounded ≠ true — but hallucination drops sharply.
Either Mode Synthetic + Computational Logic

What it's for. Reasoning over your own documents — board decks, contracts, transcripts, reports — not over the AI's public training data.

Sample Prompts "What strategic priority appears in all three decks? What appears in only one?" "What did we commit to in Q1 that we have not followed up on in Q3?" "Generate three questions a skeptical board member would ask based on these documents."
Critical Distinction
NotebookLM is grounded — answers tied to your source documents, not the public internet. Hallucination drops sharply. But grounded is not the same as true. The documents themselves may be incomplete or biased.
When NOT to use
When you need synthesis across domains the documents don't cover. NotebookLM stays inside its corpus.
07

Reasoning · Long Thinking

Trade speed for depth — when the problem deserves it.
Production Mode Sequential + Critical Thinking

Reasoning models trade speed for depth. Regular model = 2 seconds. Reasoning model = 30 seconds to 2 minutes. Use them when the problem deserves it — never when it doesn't.

Use Reasoning When
  • → Multi-step logical problems
  • → Trade-off analysis with constraints
  • → Financial modeling, scenarios
  • → Slow-but-correct is acceptable
Don't Use Reasoning When
  • → Drafting and summarizing
  • → Simple Q&A
  • → Time-sensitive work
  • → Creative brainstorming
Sample Reasoning Prompt We are considering three options for entering Vietnam: (a) acquire a local mid-tier player at ~USD 80M, (b) joint venture with an established Vietnamese conglomerate, (c) organic entry via wholly-owned subsidiary. Our advantages: brand, capital, regional ops experience. Our gaps: local regulatory navigation, distribution depth, talent. Reason through which option best matches our profile. Show your reasoning, then conclude.
Landing Line
Long thinking is not better thinking. Long thinking is deeper thinking when the problem deserves depth. Match the tool to the task.
05 · Block 03 · Deeper

Algorithmic Empathy

The foundation under every use case. Get this wrong and everything underperforms. Get this right and everything else sharpens. Algorithmic Empathy is two stances at the same time — and two micro-skills that operationalize them.

The Dual Stance

Most leaders pick one stance and fail. You need both — simultaneously.

01

Talk to it like a human

because it is NLP
  • No special syntax required
  • No programming mindset needed
  • Natural language, full sentences
  • Brief it like a smart new hire
  • Conversational, not keyword-based
02

Remember it is AI

because it is LLM
  • World knowledge — but no org knowledge
  • Probabilistic, not deterministic
  • No persistent memory across sessions
  • Confident-but-not-always-right
  • Pattern over truth
The Discipline
Stance 1 keeps you natural. Stance 2 keeps you skeptical. Both must be active at every moment — while you write the prompt and while you read the output.

The Five LLM-Character Facts

Stance 2 in detail. Hold all five in your mind as you read every AI output.

FACT 01
World knowledge — but no org knowledge
It doesn't know your company, team, KPIs, history. You must supply this.
FACT 02
Probabilistic, not deterministic
Same prompt → different answers. Rerun if needed. Not a bug.
FACT 03
No persistent memory
It forgets when the window closes. Re-supply context every session.
FACT 04
Confident-but-not-always-right
It can sound certain while being wrong. Confidence ≠ evidence.
FACT 05
Pattern over truth
It returns what looks right based on patterns. "Looks right" ≠ "is right."

Sequential Thinking — Micro-Skill #1

Don't ask a bigger question. Ask a sequence of cleaner ones. Decompose your intent into ordered steps the model can execute cleanly.

Bad — The Pile-On
Analyze our Q3 numbers, identify
weaknesses, propose three strategic
responses, prioritize them, and draft
the executive summary.
Good — Sequenced
Step 1: Read these Q3 numbers. Identify
        the three weakest indicators.
        Show your reasoning.
        [verify before continuing]

Step 2: For each, generate two
        strategic response options.
        [verify before continuing]

Step 3: Help me prioritize. What
        criteria would you recommend?
        [apply criteria]

Step 4: Draft the executive summary.
The Principle
You don't get a better answer by asking a bigger question. You get a better answer by asking a sequence of cleaner ones.

Computational Logic — Micro-Skill #2

Sequential Thinking is HOW you structure the prompt. Computational Logic is WHY structure matters — five facts about how the machine actually works.

FACT 01
Context window is finite
Long documents truncate. Old conversation gets forgotten. Front-load what matters.
FACT 02
Attention is uneven
Model attends more to start and end. Put critical instructions top or bottom — never buried.
FACT 03
Tokens, not words
Padding, redundancy, ornate language all consume budget. Be precise.
FACT 04
One token at a time
Model can't look ahead. Bad early framing locks in bad later output.
FACT 05
Probabilistic sampling
Same prompt → different outputs. Re-run borderline cases before iterating.
Leader's Checklist — Computational Logic in Practice
  • Critical instruction at the top OR bottom of the prompt
  • Cut ornate language — direct beats elegant
  • Long conversations: summarize and re-anchor every 10–15 turns
  • Complex tasks: separate conversations beat one long thread
  • Borderline output: re-run before iterating
Why This Replaces First Principles
First Principles is universal cognitive hygiene. Computational Logic is specific to how the LLM machine processes what you write. For working with AI, the LLM-specific version is sharper and more actionable.
06 · Block 04 · Close

Three Pitfalls

Even with the lens, the use cases, and Algorithmic Empathy in hand — three things will still trip you up. Two are what AI does to you. The third is your defense.

01
The Problem

Sycophancy

AI agrees with you — not because you're right, but because agreement is the path of least resistance. The model is trained to be helpful, polite, accommodating.

02
The Problem

AI Slop

Content that sounds professional but says nothing. Generic frameworks. Plausible recommendations that could apply to any company in any industry. AI hitting the safe average.

03
The Defense

AI Critique

Give the AI explicit permission and instruction to be critical and constructive. Default helpfulness is the enemy of sharp feedback. Reassign the role.

Pitfall 01 — Sycophancy

You are senior. People around you already filter their pushback. Now you have an AI that does the same. Echo chamber with infinite patience.

The Pattern
→ You propose a strategy → AI lists strengths
→ AI mentions "a few considerations" mildly
→ AI affirms your direction
→ You hint at preference → AI's "balanced view" leans that way
The Quick Test
"Where am I wrong here?"

"What's the strongest case against this?"

"You've been agreeing too much. What are you holding back?"

Pitfall 02 — AI Slop

AI Slop is the default. Sharp output is the exception you have to actively pull out of the model.

Phrases to Flag
✗ "It's important to consider..."
✗ "A multi-faceted approach..."
✗ "Stakeholder engagement..."
✗ "Best practices suggest..."
✗ "Various factors come into play..."
✗ "In today's evolving landscape..."
✗ "Key considerations include..."
Three Defense Moves
  • 01. Demand specifics — three examples with numbers
  • 02. Reject the safe answer — "Make it specific to our context"
  • 03. Force the choice — "Pick one and defend it"

Pitfall 03 — AI Critique · Five Techniques

Sycophancy is AI being too nice. AI Slop is AI being too safe. The defense against both is the same: make pushing back its job. Reassign the role.

TECHNIQUE 01

Adversarial Role Assignment

Act as a skeptical board member who rejects weak reasoning. You are not here to help me feel good. You are here to find the holes. Read my proposal below and tell me where it falls apart. [paste proposal]
TECHNIQUE 02

Explicit Steelman

Make the strongest possible case against my plan. Not a balanced view — the strongest case against. Argue it as if your job is to kill the plan. [paste plan]
TECHNIQUE 03

Pre-Mortem Reversal

Assume my strategy failed catastrophically six months from now. Work backwards. What are the three most likely failure causes? Which assumption was the fatal one? [paste strategy]
TECHNIQUE 04

Multi-Perspective Forced Disagreement

Give me three experts arguing about this: - A growth-focused CMO who wants to accelerate - A risk-focused CFO who wants to slow down - A skeptical board chair who thinks we're solving the wrong problem Surface their disagreements. Don't reconcile. [paste decision]
TECHNIQUE 05

Reverse Burden

I am going to defend a position. Your job is to attack it ruthlessly. Find every weakness. Don't be polite. Don't balance. Attack. Position: [your position]
The Closing Insight
You cannot have AI as a thought partner without giving it permission to disagree with you. AI Critique is not optional — it is how you make AI honest.

Three Things to Take With You

The Lens
Production or Exploration?
Always know your mode. Same person, same hour — different task means different mode. Trust Filter in play?
The Discipline
Algorithmic Empathy
Talk naturally — but hold the LLM character in mind. Sequential Thinking to structure. Computational Logic to respect the machine.
The Defense
AI Critique
AI will be sycophantic. AI will produce Slop. Your job: make AI honest by giving it the role.
The Closing Line
Thinking with AI is not letting AI think for you. It is structuring the conversation, applying domain reality checks, and being the critic that AI cannot be of itself. The exponent in BI = DX × (CAI)APT is not the AI. It is you.

Reusable Prompt Templates

Six templates to copy into your notes app, your AI chat history, or your team's shared library. Use them for the next 7 days — and beyond.

TEMPLATE 01

A.C.T.I.O.N.S. Scaffold

The structural backbone for any quality prompt. Fill in every element — especially Intention and Negative Constraints, which leaders most often skip.

ACTOR: You are [specific role with specific background] CONTEXT: [what the LLM doesn't know on its own — org, market, prior decisions, constraints] TASK: [one clear verb + object] INTENTION: This serves [the decision / outcome it's for] OUTPUT: [format, length, structure] NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS: Do not [things to avoid] SEQUENCE: [single-shot OR step-by-step plan] --- [your actual content / question / material]
TEMPLATE 02

Three Iteration Moves

After any first draft. Run these in sequence to push output from competent to excellent.

MOVE 1 — Specificity push: "This is good but generic. Make it specific to [your context]. Cut [the corporate jargon / hedging / generic frameworks]." MOVE 2 — Chain-of-Thought: "Walk me through your reasoning step by step before giving the final answer." MOVE 3 — Forced disagreement: "Now argue against your own recommendation. Give me the three strongest counter-arguments."
TEMPLATE 03

Five-Step Decision Flow

For high-stakes decisions. The full-stack synthesis — Context Payload, A.C.T.I.O.N.S., Iteration, Four Filters, Decision.

STEP 1 — Build Context Payload: "Here is everything you need to know to help me decide on [X]: - Market context: [...] - Internal capability: [...] - Constraints: [...] - Strategic intent: [...]" STEP 2 — A.C.T.I.O.N.S. prompt: [apply Template 01] STEP 3 — Iteration Loop: [apply Template 02] STEP 4 — Apply Four Filters: - RELEVANCE: Does this fit my org's actual reality? - EXCELLENCE: Is this world-class or just competent? - REALITY: Is this operationally possible? - TRUST: Where does AI stop and I take over? STEP 5 — Decide and own it.

Sycophancy Quick Test

When you suspect you're being agreed with too easily. Paste any of these into the conversation. If new critiques suddenly appear — sycophancy was active.
